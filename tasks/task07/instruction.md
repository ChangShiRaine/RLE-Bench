# Clear the bin

A green KLT bin arrives at your robot cell holding 12–16 stamped steel
U-brackets, dumped loose. A conveyor runs along the other side of the cell.
Your job: ship the software that empties the bin onto the conveyor —
**as many parts as possible, as fast as possible, breaking nothing**.

The cell is simulated in MuJoCo (deterministic, headless). You are free to
use ANY method you can build from this environment: classical depth
processing, scripted state machines, motion planning, learned policies —
anything that fits the I/O contract below.

## The cell

- **Robot**: Franka Panda (7 joints, position actuators) on a pedestal,
  carrying a **contact-electromagnet** on a slim extension probe. While the
  magnet is energized (`ctrl[7] >= 128`), any part whose surface touches the
  head is held rigidly; several nested parts can be held at once. Dropping
  the command below 128 releases everything instantly. Nothing is attracted
  at a distance — the face must touch.
- **Bin**: interior 360 × 260 × 190 mm, bolted to a 100 mm stand. It cannot
  be moved or tipped.
- **Conveyor & drop zone**: a belt across the cell with a marked drop zone.
  A part that comes to rest inside the zone after being released is **cleared** and immediately carried away by the belt. 
- **The drop zone is a small placement nest (14 x 14 cm) and it takes ONE
  part at a time** (the downstream station expects singulated parts).
  While two or more uncleared parts occupy the nest, NONE of them counts —
  a double delivery blocks it until you lift the extra back off. The rest
  of the belt is dead surface: parts left there earn nothing (but can be
  re-picked). The magnet grabs stacks happily (nested parts stick
  together).
  Parts that end up on the workcell floor are lost — and penalized.
- **Cameras**: a fixed overhead RGB-D camera above the bin and a wrist RGB-D
  camera on the tool mount, both 10 Hz. Calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics are published. Ground-truth part poses are never available.
- **Parts**: identical stamped U-channel brackets (72 × 42 × 37 mm, 5 mm
  gauge steel, two flared flanges). Each episode's pile is generated by a
  seeded drop-and-settle; `harness/episodes.py` gives you
  unlimited practice piles on the public design seeds. Evaluation uses
  unpublished seeds from the same distribution.

Public geometry and timing are documented in `harness/spec.py`. Use the
`cell_spec` dict passed to `reset()` for policy parameters.

## Deliverable and I/O contract

Ship a **policy package** to `/logs/artifacts/policy/`:

```
/logs/artifacts/policy/
├── policy.py         # required — see contract
└── ...               # anything else it needs (weights, data, modules)
```

**Stage early, stage often.** You have **4 hours** of session time,
budget your experiments accordingly. Copy your current best policy into
`/logs/artifacts/policy/` the moment it works at all. An unstaged policy in your workspace scores zero.

`policy.py` must expose:

```python
def make_policy():
    return obj          # with:
    #   obj.reset(cell_spec: dict, seed: int) -> None    once per episode
    #   obj.act(obs: dict) -> array of 8 floats          at 20 Hz, in order
```

`obs` every tick: `qpos` (7,), `qvel` (7,), `tau` (7,), `ft` (6 — wrist
force-torque at the tool mount: force N + torque Nm in the site frame,
carrying tool weight, payload and every contact, with mild seeded sensor
noise), `mag_on` (0/1), `t` (sim seconds). On 10 Hz frame ticks additionally:
`overhead_rgb` (480×640×3 uint8), `overhead_depth` (480×640 float32,
NaN = dropout), `wrist_rgb`, `wrist_depth` (240×320),
`wrist_T_world_cam` (4×4).

Return `ctrl`: 7 joint position targets (clamped to actuator ranges) + the
magnet command 0–255. Non-finite values or wrong shapes count as faults
(the last valid command is held).

`cell_spec` carries the camera calibration, bin/zone geometry, rates,
magnet data, the damage-force limit, and `model_file` — a compiled MuJoCo
model of the cell (arm + statics, no parts) provided at that path for
your own FK/IK/planning.

The `reset()` seed is exclusively for your policy's deterministic RNG. It is
independent of, and cannot be used to reconstruct, the episode's pile seed.

## Scoring Tips

- Initial part of the pile is easy, the goal is to clearing all the parts.
- Avoid hitting the bin, dropping parts on the floor, double pick, etc.
- `damage_events` — a part experiencing sustained contact force above the
  published limit (`cell_spec["damage_force_n"]`, 2 consecutive control
  ticks). Tossing parts toward the belt is legal; denting them is not.

Compute: sim time is decoupled from wall time — the sim waits for `act()`.
Per episode you have a cumulative 360 s submission-compute budget, measured
on the verifier's clock while it waits for `reset()` and `act()`. MuJoCo
stepping, RGB-D rendering, and diagnostic video are excluded. Each `act()`
also has a 30 s hang cap. Evaluation is CPU only.

## Developing

```bash
cd /workspace
python3 dev_runner.py my_policy_dir                    # representative 12-part pile
python3 dev_runner.py my_policy_dir --seed 307 --budget 60
python3 dev_runner.py my_policy_dir --seed 101 --video /logs/run.mp4
```

The dev runner uses the same isolated policy subprocess as evaluation:
`PYTHONPATH` is cleared, the working directory is temporary, and root-run
containers drop the policy to uid/gid 65534 with no supplementary groups.
Only your policy package and installed dependencies are on its import path;
workspace `harness` imports are unavailable. Include all custom modules,
weights and data as regular files in the submitted package (no symbolic
links), and resolve files relative to
`__file__`. Read public parameters and `model_file` from `cell_spec`;
do not depend on workspace or verifier paths. The package is copied into a
temporary readable directory; the original is not modified. Import or factory
failure stops development and verification before any episode runs.

Every completed dev run includes a `penalty_events` array. Damage entries
identify the part, strongest contact pair and contact point, motion state,
and current/previous sustained force. Floor-drop entries identify the part's
threshold-crossing pose and velocity, current contacts, and most recent magnet
release. Use these event records together with the aggregate penalty counts to
diagnose the cause rather than guessing from the final scene.

The public practice battery is stratified by part count and pile complexity:
`35` is a representative 12-part pile, `16` a representative 13-part pile,
`307` a lower-contact 14-part pile, `211` a denser 15-part pile, and `101` the
dense, tilted 16-part stress case. Do not treat 101 alone as representative.
All five seeds are yours; generate more piles with
`harness.episodes.pile_poses(seed)` for anything you train.
The dev runner executes the exact evaluation loop (same physics, same
sensors, same latch rules) and prints the same metrics the verifier
computes — on your seeds instead of the hidden ones.

Everything you need is already in this container (numpy, scipy, mujoco,
torch-cpu, opencv). There is no network access.
